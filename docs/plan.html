1. Question and audience

Research question: How can an early-career Enterprise AI student present trustworthy evidence of technical work while comparing Forward Deployed Engineering and Software Engineering?

Audience: internship recruiters, mentors, classmates, and the instructor. These are proposed personas, not surveyed users. A recruiter may want to find concrete work quickly; a classmate may want to trace a claim to a source; a mentor may want to distinguish current skills from future goals. These needs must be tested with real readers.

2. Observations versus assumptions

Source-supported observations

The Baidu FDE internship posting describes customer-facing AI/API integration, while the C3 AI Software Engineer internship describes platform software development. NIST's AI Risk Management Framework and OWASP's Top 10 provide additional background guidance. See original sources and limitations on the research page.

Persona assumptions to test

Assumption A: recruiters prefer a project link above the fold. Assumption B: peers understand a short skills list without supporting evidence. Assumption C: a mobile menu is easy to use. These are hypotheses, not research findings.

3. Compare alternatives before building

Option A — Project-first

Show work immediately, followed by context and research. Advantage: direct evidence. Risk: incomplete case studies become prominent.

Option B — Career-story-first

Explain interests and goals before projects. Advantage: understandable narrative. Risk: delays access to evidence.

Chosen prototype — Hybrid

Start with a short career statement, then skills and clearly labeled project cards, followed by research and documentation. This is an initial design decision, not an A/B test result.

Basis: the November 10 course clarification calls for original-source inspection and traceable decisions.

5. User stories and acceptance checks

  1. Recruiter: I can identify the student's direction in under a minute. Check the career section names Forward Deployed Engineering and Software Engineering without unsupported expertise claims.
  2. Reviewer: I can find an actual repository. Check that at least one project card opens a real repository.
  3. Classmate: I can inspect the original evidence. Check that the research page names the question, contains working original-source links, and separates evidence from inference.
  4. Mobile reader: I can navigate using a narrow viewport and keyboard. Check the menu opens, closes, and reaches all sections.
  5. Mentor: I can tell what is completed versus planned. Check that incomplete case studies are labeled as in progress.
