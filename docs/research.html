IS117 / PUBLIC RESEARCH EVIDENCE

Career research: claims, sources, decisions

Question: How can an early-career AI student present trustworthy evidence of technical work while exploring AI engineering and cybersecurity?

These sources inform project design and learning priorities. They are not proof of internship eligibility, current openings, or completed technical competence.

Original sources

Source A — NIST AI Risk Management Framework

Open original NIST AI RMF page ↗

Observation: NIST publishes a framework for managing AI risks and improving trustworthiness. Inference: a useful student AI case study should include evaluation, limitations, and clear evidence rather than only a demo.

Feature: planned AI project template with a testing/limitations section. Check: a reviewer can find linked tests and limitations once that case study is built.

Source B — OWASP Top 10

Open original OWASP Top 10 page ↗

Observation: OWASP documents categories of important web application security risks. Inference: cybersecurity work should explain scope and evidence carefully, not imply expertise from interest alone.

Feature: honest security learning goals and future authorized lab write-up. Check: the site avoids unverified security credentials or unsanctioned testing claims.

Compare the two career directions

Shared employer evidence: both roles involve programming, collaboration, technical documentation, testing or verification, and careful review of AI-assisted work. Difference: Amazon emphasizes building and operating software systems; Atlassian emphasizes security investigation and protective tools. My decision: explore AI/software engineering as a primary direction while investigating cybersecurity as a potential specialization. This is a personal research conclusion, not a statement from either employer.
